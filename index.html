What we believe

Empowerment is not a privilege.
It is a right.

Women's empowerment means ensuring women and girls have the rights, resources, confidence, and opportunities to make decisions about their own lives. It means removing barriers—not asking women to work harder just to be treated equally.

The central issue

Gender inequality still
holds women back.

Unequal treatment can appear in everyday expectations, classrooms, workplaces, and leadership. Recognizing it is the first step toward changing it.

↗01 / Opportunity

Unequal opportunities

Bias and structural barriers can limit women's access to jobs, promotion, training, and financial resources.

↗
◉02 / Representation

Missing voices

When women are underrepresented in decision-making, communities lose important experiences and perspectives.

✳03 / Stereotypes

Limiting labels

Ideas about what women “should” do can restrict choices, confidence, and freedom to pursue ambitions.

♡04 / Unpaid care

Invisible work

Unequal expectations around household and caregiving duties can reduce time for education, rest, and paid work.

The numbers matter

Look beyond the headlines.
See the facts.

Gender equality can be measured. These global indicators show why continued action and reliable data matter.

▥
27.2%

Women in national parliaments

Women held about 27.2% of seats in national parliaments worldwide on 1 January 2024.

Source: UN Women, 2024 ↗
◷
2.5×

More unpaid care work

Women perform around 2.5 times as much unpaid care and domestic work as men, limiting time for other opportunities.

Source: UN Women ↗
↗
20%

Global gender pay gap

The ILO has reported that women globally earn around 20% less than men on average, although estimates vary by measure and year.

Source: International Labour Organization ↗

ⓘ Global figures do not describe every country or individual experience. Always check each source for its methodology, data year, and latest updates.

Know your rights

Progress needs
protection.

Philippine laws provide important protections and promote women's participation and safety. Learn what each law addresses and consult official sources for full details.

♀
01

Magna Carta of Women

Republic Act No. 9710 — A comprehensive law recognizing women's human rights and requiring action against discrimination.

Read the law ↗
↗
02

Women in Development and Nation Building Act

Republic Act No. 7192 — Supports women's participation and equal opportunities in development.

Read the law ↗
↗
03

Anti-Sexual Harassment Act of 1995

Republic Act No. 7877 — Addresses sexual harassment in employment, education, and training environments covered by the law.

Read the law ↗
↗
04

Safe Spaces Act

Republic Act No. 11313 — Addresses gender-based sexual harassment in public spaces, online spaces, workplaces, and educational or training institutions.

Read the law ↗
↗
05

Anti-Violence Against Women and Their Children Act

Republic Act No. 9262 — Provides legal protection against covered forms of violence against women and their children.

Read the law ↗
